Crème

Brighter highs that don't get harsh

Crème takes a copy of the top end of your track, crushes its spiky peaks and blends it back in under the original. The top gets fuller and silkier without getting harsher, and the transients stay as they were.

VST3 · macOS · Windows

How it works

Engineers do this on a parallel bus

They send the track to a second channel, cut everything there but the top, crush that copy with a very fast limiter and blend it in under the dry track. The spiky peaks get flattened, so the lift goes into the body and tail of each sound instead of onto its sharp edge.

Crème does all of that in one plug-in, with no send to set up. Put it straight on a vocal, the drums, a bus or the master.

01

Put it last

Insert it after compression and de-essing. A lift makes sibilance and harshness worse, so deal with those first.

02

Pick a zone

Click CREAM for a silky top from 4 to 16 kHz. Click AIR for air above 10 kHz without touching the upper mids.

03

Use a little

Each 10% of Crème is about 1 dB of lift on steady material. Try 10 to 20% on a mix bus and 15 to 30% on a vocal.

04

Match the level

Brighter sounds better when it's also louder. Pull Output down until bypassing Crème doesn't change the loudness, then judge.

Choose which part of the top it works on

Two handles on the Range slider set the band Crème works on, anywhere from 1 to 20 kHz. Below 4 kHz you're getting into the 2 to 5 kHz area where harshness lives, so use less there and turn Smooth on.

A meter that tells you when to stop

The Balance meter shows how bright the band you're lifting is compared with a balanced top end for this track. At DULL there's room for more. Turn Crème up until the marker reaches BALANCED and glows.

If it carries on into amber and then red, the top is getting harsh. Back off a little.

Lift the top only where it needs it

Smooth is a dynamic EQ on what Crème adds. Its twelve bands hold the lift back wherever it would push the top past balanced, and on piercing notes and hard "s" sounds. Everything else keeps the full lift.

It never cuts the original signal, and it's off until you switch it on. The lights under the switch show how much it's holding back.

Controls

Crème
How much of the crushed top goes under the original. At 0% the output is the input, bit for bit.
Delta
Plays only what Crème adds, like soloing the parallel bus. It should sound smooth, not fizzy.

FAQ

Questions

Which DAWs does it work in?

Any DAW that loads VST3 plug-ins, on macOS or Windows.

Will it work in Logic or Pro Tools?

No. Logic only loads Audio Units and Pro Tools only loads AAX. Crème is VST3.

How is it different from a high shelf?

A shelf turns up everything up there, spikes included, which is why an EQ boost can sound thin or harsh. Crème crushes the spikes in its copy, so on a drum loop the peaks of the hits rise only about 1 dB while the rest gets the full lift. Quiet detail like breaths, the room and reverb tails isn't crushed, so it gets the full lift too.

Why not just do it on a parallel bus?

You can, but it takes a send, an EQ and a limiter, and the filtered copy can cancel against the dry track and thin the sound out where the two meet. Crème's copy lines up with the original, so that doesn't happen, and it adds no latency.

Does it need an internet connection?

Only to activate or deactivate. The rest of the time it works offline and sends nothing.

Can I move it to a new computer?

Yes. On the old one, open the gear menu and choose Deactivate this computer, then activate the new one. If the old computer is gone, email support@anymeansaudio.com.
